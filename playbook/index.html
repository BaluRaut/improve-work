From a business request to a released feature

One path for every new feature: who does what at each stage, which document they write, and how to keep it all in Confluence. Small changes take the short route.

Where do I start?With the next feature in your backlog. Its requestor fills in a one-page brief.
Who writes the technical doc?The feature owner, the engineer leading the feature. The tech lead coaches and approves.
One Confluence page or many?One parent page per feature, with each document as a child page under it.

Right-size it

How much paperwork a change needs

The product owner picks the size at triage. Use the lightest size that is still safe.

SizeUse it whenBriefRFCDelivery PlanJiraReview
LiteSmall, low-risk, one team. No architecture, data, security or ops impact.A few lines in the Jira storyNot neededNot neededStory with acceptance criteria and test notesTech lead
StandardA real feature: several stories, some technical choices, one product area.YesRequired sections plus the ones that applyYesEpic and storiesPicked by impact, from the review matrix
FullSeveral teams or products, or real risk: data migration, security, privacy, money, reliability, cost.Yes, completeFull, with tested rollout and rollbackYes, with cross-team dependenciesEpic, stories and linked epics in other teamsFull review matrix, plus an outcome review after release

Decide in four questions

Ask them in order and stop at the first yes.

  1. Does it touch login or permissions, money, personal data, or migrate production data? Full.
  2. Can it only ship if several teams deliver work that depends on each other? Full.
  3. Does it need a technical choice, a new API, table or piece of infrastructure, or more than a few stories? Standard.
  4. None of the above? Lite.

Examples

Lite a Jira story is enough

  • Show invoice dates as 22 Nov 2026 instead of 2026-11-22One screen, one team, easy to undo.
  • Add a "last updated" column to an internal admin exportInternal users only, no new data stored.
  • Add the missing loading spinner on the order history pageVisual fix, no behaviour change.
  • Raise a retry count in one service's configExisting setting, rollback is a config change.

Standard brief, RFC, plan

  • Dark mode for web and appsSeveral platforms and stories, but no data, money or security risk.
  • Add Marathi and TamilMany screens, emails and SMS, plus translation and font choices.
  • Rate limits on the partner APIOne team, but new infrastructure and partners must be told first.
  • A new filter on product search that needs a new API fieldA technical choice and a few stories, within one product area.

Full everything, plus outcome review

  • Customers cancel their own ordersAutomatic refunds touch money, and four teams must ship together.
  • Upgrade the orders database to PostgreSQL 16No visible change, but a production migration with downtime.
  • Move login to a new identity providerEvery user is affected, and a mistake locks people out.
  • Split the orders service in twoData moves between databases, and other teams' integrations change.

Cases that fool people

ChangeLooks likeActuallyWhy
Password-reset links expire after 1 hour instead of 24Lite: one lineFullIt changes authentication. Get it wrong and users are locked out, or links stay open too long.
Add the customer's phone number to an analytics eventLite: one fieldFullPersonal data is going to a third-party tool, so privacy and consent need review.
Change how refunds are roundedLite: one formulaFullIt's money. Finance has to agree, and the old and new results must reconcile.
Redesign all twelve help centre pagesFull: lots of workStandardBig, but one team, with no data, money or security impact. Size of work isn't risk.
Bump a library by a patch versionStandard: touches every serviceLiteCovered by automated tests and easy to roll back, unless it's an auth or crypto library.

Risk beats size. A one-line change to login, payments, personal data or a production migration is Full, however small it looks. If unsure between two sizes, pick the bigger one at triage; you can drop sections later with a reason.

Splitting the work

How to split a feature into work items

In stage 5 the feature owner drafts the split with the teams, and each team estimates its own items. Every item should deliver one outcome someone can see or check, fit within a sprint, have one owning team, and be safe to release on its own behind a flag.

Factors to consider

User valueSlice through UI, API and data so each item works end to end, not "backend" and "frontend" halves.
SizeFinish and verify within a sprint. Split bigger items again; merge items under half a day.
Team boundariesOne owning team per item. Across teams, agree the API or event contract first, then build in parallel.
Dependencies and orderFoundations first: schema, queues, flag, contract. Record blockers as Jira links.
Risk and unknownsRiskiest work first. Turn open questions into time-boxed spikes.
Release safetyFeature flag for unfinished work. Data changes as expand, backfill, switch over, clean up.
TestingTests go in each item's acceptance criteria. Separate QA items only for runs across many items.
Cross-cutting workEvents, translations, monitors and security go inside the item they belong to, unless another team owns them.
Outside lead timeVendors, approvals, app store review, translation review: start them early as their own items.
Parallel workSplit so people touch different screens or services and don't block each other.
Clean-upCreate items now to remove the flag and old code after launch.

Example: customers cancel their own orders

Split by layer: avoid

  • Cancel API
  • Cancel UI
  • Refund worker
  • Testing
  • Tracking
  • Translations

Nothing works until all six are done, testing and tracking come last and get cut, and two other teams' work is hidden inside "Cancel API".

Confluence

How to lay it out in Confluence

One parent page per feature, with its documents as child pages. Don't put everything on one long page, and don't keep a flat list of loose documents.

  • Engineering Deliveryspace, or a section in your team's space
    • How we deliverthe rules, read-only
      • Workflow
      • Definition of Ready
      • Definition of Done
      • Review and approval
      • Estimation guidelines
    • Featuresindex: one live table of every feature
      • FEAT-123 Order cancellationfeature hub, Standard
        • Request brief
        • RFC-101 Order cancellation
        • Delivery plan
      • FEAT-124 Postgres 16 upgradeFull
        • Request brief
        • RFC-104 Postgres 16 upgrade
        • Delivery plan
        • Outcome review
      • FEAT-130 Fix date format on invoicesLite: hub page only, or just Jira
    • Archivemove closed features here

Why child pages, not one big page

  1. Different people write them at different times. The requestor writes the brief in week one; the feature owner writes the RFC weeks later. Separate pages keep separate history and comments.
  2. Each document is approved on its own. On one page it is unclear whether the RFC or the plan was approved.
  3. Different readers. Business people read the hub and the brief. Engineers read the RFC and the plan.
  4. An RFC alone can run to ten pages. Add the brief and the plan and nobody finishes reading.

What goes on the feature hub page

  • A Page Properties table: Feature ID, size, status, product owner, feature owner, tech lead, target release.
  • A Jira Issues macro showing the epic's stories with their live status.
  • A Children Display macro listing the brief, RFC and plan.
  • A short decision log: date, decision, who decided.

On the Features page, a Page Properties Report macro reads every hub's table and builds the index for you. Give every hub page the label feature.

Getting the templates into Confluence

  • Create four space templates: Feature hub, Request brief, RFC and Delivery plan. Then people pick them from + Create instead of copying old pages.
  • Open each template on the toolkit site, select the rendered page, copy it and paste it into the Confluence template editor. Headings and tables carry over. Don't paste the raw .md file.
  • Turn the {placeholders} into Confluence placeholder text, so they vanish as people type.
  • Copy the five guides into How we deliver once, then restrict editing to the people who own the process.
Rollout

How to start

  1. This week · about half a day

    Set up Confluence

    • Create the space, or a section in an existing one.
    • Add the four templates.
    • Copy the five guides into How we deliver.
    • Create the Features index page.
  2. Next week · 30 minutes

    Walk the leads through it

    • Invite product owners, tech leads and the engineers who will own features.
    • Go through this page together.
    • Agree who is the product owner and tech lead for each area.
    • Explain the feature owner role and agree how it rotates.
  3. Following month

    Pilot two features

    • Pick one Standard and one Lite feature from the upcoming backlog.
    • Run them through every stage.
    • Leave work that's already in progress as it is.
  4. After the pilot

    Trim, then make it the default

    • Ask what slowed people down and which sections nobody filled.
    • Cut or merge those sections.
    • From then on, no new epic without a feature hub page.
Tomorrow morning

Pick the next feature in your backlog. Create its hub page, send the brief template to its requestor, and decide which engineer will own it. That is stage 1 done.

Common questions

What people will ask

Do I need an RFC for every change?

No. Lite changes skip it and go straight to a Jira story with acceptance criteria.

Why doesn't the tech lead write the RFC?

So the engineers who build features learn to design them, and the tech lead isn't a bottleneck. The tech lead coaches, reviews section 6 early and approves.

Who fills RFC sections 7 to 15?

The engineers who own those areas. The feature owner assembles the RFC and makes sure it reads as one proposal.

Is a Rovo or AI estimate a commitment?

No. Only estimates the team gives itself count, and a requested deadline is not a commitment either.

What if scope changes halfway?

Update the RFC, record the decision in section 22, and move the feature back a stage if needed.

Where do I check status?

The stage of the feature is on its hub page. The progress of each story is in Jira.

What about urgent fixes?

Take the expedited path: write down who decided and why, and keep the security, privacy and data checks.
